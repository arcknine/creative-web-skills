# Motion principles

## Assign purpose

Classify each animation before implementation:

- **Orient:** explain spatial or navigational change.
- **Reveal:** control reading order or expose a meaningful detail.
- **Connect:** show continuity between states or sections.
- **Emphasize:** focus attention on a priority.
- **Acknowledge:** confirm input or state.
- **Atmosphere:** express material or brand character without blocking use.

If an animation fits none, remove it.

## Shape time

Use duration relative to distance, scale, and importance. Small feedback should feel immediate; large spatial changes need enough time to remain legible. Avoid applying one duration to everything.

Entrances often benefit from a strong deceleration. Exits can accelerate and finish sooner. Continuous scroll-linked motion should feel mechanically tied to scroll rather than eased independently. Choose custom eases only when their character is visible and consistent.

Sequence by hierarchy: primary signal first, support second, detail last. Keep staggers tight enough to read as a group. Long cascades make users wait. Use overlap to maintain momentum, then leave still intervals so hero moments retain force.

## Use space coherently

Derive direction from layout, navigation, or imagery. Masks suggest surfaces opening; scale suggests depth; translation suggests physical relationship. Parallax requires a believable depth hierarchy and subtle range. Preserve crisp final states and avoid transforms that degrade text rendering.

Design load, scroll, hover, focus, active, exit, and interrupted states together. Motion is successful when it makes the interface easier to read and control while giving it a specific physical character.
